## 1. The shape

Every lane runs the same five steps. **Main CTA:** book a free strategy call (cal.com/rezkarim/deck-call; rename the event title to "Strategy call" so it fits every service, and keep the link). **Soft CTA** for people who aren't ready: a free template (#6, #7) or the scorecard (#5).

| Step | What happens | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Attract | Someone meets your work | Warm messages, outreach, LinkedIn, Behance, directories, marketplaces, Google |
| Land | They open the page made for them | A portfolio page, /work-with-me, /agencies, an agency service page |
| Capture | They book a call, reply, or leave an email | Cal.com, a reply, Gumroad, the agency quote form |
| Convert | A call, a proposal the same day, 50% upfront | 03-playbook §1 and §3 |
| Expand | Monthly plan, retainer, recommendation, intros | At every delivery (§9) |

## 2. Hormozi's rules we use

Paraphrased from *$100M Leads* and *$100M Offers*; sources at the end.

- **A lead magnet** is a free, complete fix for one narrow problem. Fixing it reveals the next problem, which the paid offer fixes.
- **Three types:** reveal the problem (a diagnosis) · a sample or trial · one step of the process (a template, tool or checklist).
- **Making one:** pick the problem and the person → the type → the format (software, information, a service, a physical product) → test the name → make it easy to use → make it good enough to pay for → make the next step obvious.
- **Four ways to get leads:** warm outreach, free content, cold outreach, paid ads. Start warm; early on, do free work for a few people in return for testimonials.
- **Offers:** value = (dream outcome × how likely they'll get it) ÷ (time it takes × effort for them). Strengthen an offer with scarcity, urgency, bonuses, guarantees and a clear name, not with discounts.
- Done-by-you magnets convert best but cost hours, so use them one-to-one. Self-serve tools and files are for content, where volume matters.

## 4. The 7 lead magnets

| # | Who | Lead magnet (working name) | Small problem it fixes | Bigger problem it reveals | Type and format | Limit | Made by |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Past clients | Free LinkedIn Refresh | Their profile looks dated | Their company's brand or website now looks dated next to it | Sample, done by you | 5 this month | You ([04](04-lead-magnet-linkedin-refresh.md)) |
| 2 | Teams at your friends' MNCs | Free Makeover: one slide, one post, or one website section | One weak piece of their material | The rest no longer matches | Sample, done by you | 3 a week | You or the designer |
| 3 | Agencies | Pilot Brief: pay only if you use the work | "Can we trust a new partner?" | They need this capacity every month | Trial with a guarantee | 2 a month | You and the team |
| 4 | Funded startups (best leads) | Cold-Read Test: 3 fixes in a 3-minute video | They can't see what a stranger sees | The full fix is a paid project | Diagnosis, a Loom video by you | 3 a week | You (uses the lead engine's Loom prep) |
| 5 | LinkedIn and site visitors | Cold-Read Scorecard: 12 checks across brand, website and deck | "Is my first impression costing me clients?" | A low score, and you sell the fix | Diagnosis, a page on your site | None | Claude builds it |
| 6 | Founders | 10-Slide Story Deck | Starting a deck from a blank page | It still needs their brand and real design | One step, a template | None | Your designer |
| 7 | Founders and executives | LinkedIn Profile Kit (free Canva template) | A plain LinkedIn profile | DIY looks fine, but not premium | One step, a template | None | Your designer |

The Cold-Read Test is your named method across brand, websites and decks: could a stranger tell what you do, and trust you, within seconds? #4 is the personal version and #5 the self-serve one.

**Start order**
1. This week, nothing to build: #1 (Sam is under way), #2 (your MNC list), #4 (the send block), #3 (the Friday agency messages).
2. While the sites are built: #6 and #7, from the designer's template line.
3. Last: #5, the scorecard page, built with the portfolio.

## 6. Message to friends at MNCs

Written to your message rules: short, no colon, semicolon, dash or hyphen, signed Rezaul.

```text
Hi [Name], hope all's well at [Company]!
Quick one. I design brands, websites, social content and presentations for B2B teams, with a small team behind me. Recent work includes SolVita's brand and website, and the deck Earworm used in its £200K funding round.
Does your team ever bring in outside help for that kind of work? If so, who would be the right person to talk to?
Happy to make over one slide, one post or one section of your website for free, anything that isn't confidential, so you can see the difference.
Rezaul
```

Past-client messages are in [04](04-lead-magnet-linkedin-refresh.md).

## 7. Email

- Magnets #6 and #7 sit on Gumroad at $0+, which collects the email. Your portfolio's /free page explains them and links there. On LinkedIn, offer them in posts and send the link by DM after a real comment (gated on the site, ungated in DMs).
- Gumroad's built-in emails carry the sequence (check the automation options at setup; no new subscription):

## 8. Content engine

- Runs through the post pipeline (02-plan step 21) once your portfolio is live. Claude drafts and schedules; you approve each batch and click Schedule on LinkedIn.
- LinkedIn: 3 posts a week. About 70–80% teaching (before/after fixes across brand, web, social and decks; the Cold-Read Test), 20–30% story, and about 1 sales post in every 8–10.
- Each post points to one place: your profile (then the portfolio) or one magnet.
- Proof posts: before/after LinkedIn refreshes, only with the client's OK.
- Behance: 1–2 cases a week, each ending with the call and your portfolio link. Pinterest: template pins ([05](05-templates.md)). X, Facebook, Instagram: cross-posts only.

## 9. Proof loop, at every delivery

1. Acceptance and payment.
2. A LinkedIn recommendation, and the OK to quote it on the case study.
3. The OK to show the work, or to label it "client under NDA".
4. An intro ask while they're happiest.
5. Offer the monthly plan.
