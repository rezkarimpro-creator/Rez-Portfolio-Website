## 1. Job

- Rank for "hire a team" searches: services, industries, prices, guides, and later templates.
- Look like a real studio to bigger buyers (packages from $3k) and to agencies looking for white-label help.
- Turn visits into quote requests and project calls.

## 2. Name and look

- **DashBloom is dropped** from this site: new name, new look.
- **Name test:** short; easy to say and spell in English; the .com (or a strong alternative) is free; not a design cliché; fits both design and build; no "DashBloom". Claude can shortlist 10 with free domains when you ask.
- **dashbloom.design:** decide later (redirect it to the new domain, or keep it).
- **Legal entity on invoices:** DashBloom LLC trading under the new name, or a new company. Decide before the first invoice.

**URLs:** `/{service}/` and `/{service}/{industry}/` (for example /pitch-deck-design/saas/) · `/industries/{industry}/` · `/work/{case}/` · `/guides/{slug}/` · `/templates/{slug}/` (phase 2) · `/glossary/{term}/`. Lowercase, hyphens, no dates. One URL per search intent: never both /logo-design/healthcare/ and /medical-logo-design/.

**No city or "near me" pages.** Google treats pages that only swap the place name as doorway pages.

## 9. What each page type holds

**Service × industry page** (most of the 1,000):
1. H1 in plain words: "Website design for law firms".
2. A 2–3 sentence answer first: what they get, typical price, timeline. Search snippets and AI answers quote this part.
3. Three problems specific to the industry. Examples: law firm sites must win trust in seconds and turn visits into consultations; healthcare brands carry regulatory and clinical weight; SaaS investors read the metrics slide first.
4. What's delivered, and in which formats.
5. Proof: the closest case, labelled client or concept, with 1–2 images.
6. The questions this industry's buyers ask (customers for brands, visitors for websites, investors for decks).
7. Process (4 steps) and timeline.
8. Price range, the same numbers as /pricing.
9. FAQ: 4–6 questions written for this page only.
10. Related: the industry hub, the service page, 1 guide, 1 template (phase 2).
11. CTA: Get a quote · Book a project call.

**Service page:** what it is, who it's for, deliverables, process, price range, proof grid, industries served (links), FAQ, CTA.
**Industry hub:** the industry's design problems, the services for it (links), proof, a guide, CTA.
**Case study:** the studio write-up (problem, team, process, decisions, result), not the portfolio's text.
**Guide:** answers one question fully, with your named method where it fits (the Cold-Read Test), a price table where relevant, and a CTA.
**Template (phase 2):** previews, formats, what's included, links to its listings, "Want us to customize it?".

## 10. Quality rules and gates

Why 1,000 pages won't sink the site:
- Google's spam policies (March 2024) target scaled content abuse: many pages made mainly to rank, with little value to readers, whether people or AI wrote them. Pages that only swap one word are the classic case.
- Every page carries at least 3 blocks written for that page alone (problems, questions, proof, FAQ) and one real visual (a case or a template). No proof or unique answer, no page.
- No invented stats, reviews, client logos or results. Concepts are labelled.
- Phase 1 goes out in two batches: A (core, services, hubs, cases), then B (service × industry, guides) 1–2 weeks later. Indexing is checked weekly.
- Pages with no impressions after 90 days get improved, merged or removed.

| Gate | When | Test |
|---|---|---|
| G2 · phase 1 live | When your friend has built it | Agreement signed (for build pages) · 1 approved page per type · every claim checked · the voice change logged in 04-log |
| G3 · phase 2 | 4–6 weeks after launch | ≥ 80% of submitted pages indexed (Search Console, Pages report) · impressions up 3 weeks running · no manual action |
| G4 · phase 3 | When phase 2 pages earn clicks | Organic quote requests arriving · grow only the page types that earn impressions |

G1 is the portfolio's go-live ([portfolio spec §10](../01-portfolio/01-portfolio-spec.md#10-done)).

## 13. Dev partner agreement

One page, signed before any build page goes live:
- **Scope he delivers:** websites, landing pages, CMS builds (Webflow, WordPress, Framer, headless), Shopify if he confirms, and the studio's own site.
- **Roles:** who quotes, who talks to the client (one project lead), who owns code and accounts (the client, after payment).
- **Money:** a share of each build's fee, or his rate plus a margin. He's paid when the client pays.
- **Delivery:** turnaround, a support window, who fixes bugs.
- **Proof:** 2–3 of his builds shown as studio work, with his OK.
- **On the site:** Engineering Lead, with his OK on name and photo.
